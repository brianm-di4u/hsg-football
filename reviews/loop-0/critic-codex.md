# Critic B (Codex, GPT-6.1 Sol, effort high): loop 0

Continue. The legacy tier reads as a functional football game with recognizable school colors, clear field markings, and a useful responsive interface, but it falls below every rubric gate. The largest deficits are primitive player construction, weak physical contact, and lighting that separates a bright field from an almost empty black venue without convincingly grounding the athletes. The gap also exists against the indie benchmarks: Legend Bowl achieves stronger visual consistency and Retro Bowl gives its action more useful screen space. The desktop HUD is the strongest component; the close-ups and motion strips are the weakest.

Capture paths below are relative to `games/football/screenshots/review-loop-0/`; benchmark filenames refer to `games/football/benchmarks/`.

1. **Art — 3.5/10.** `crops/legacy/R04-slot_C.png` exposes angular torso junctions, cylinder shins, rectangular hands, minimal footwear, and identical-looking equipment surfaces. `crops/legacy/R03-slot_QB.png` shows similarly primitive officials and nearly interchangeable player proportions. Numbers, uniform colors, shoulder pads, and basic facemasks provide football identity, but helmet and jersey shading lack convincing material separation. `crops/legacy/R01-crowd.png` is visibly a collection of boxes and diamond-shaped heads. Turf has texture, but that does not lift the overall asset quality to the rubric's clean, consistent 5. Compared with `axis-3.jpg`, the players lack anatomical and equipment definition; compared with `legendbowl-1.jpg`, the simplification is less cohesive.

2. **Lighting & atmosphere — 4.5/10.** `legacy/desktop/R20.png` has an effective dusk palette, visible lamp halos, and haze that suggests stadium illumination. `legacy/desktop/R01.png` establishes a lit playing surface surrounded by darkness. However, the field's broad light pools and diffuse shadows do not convey distinct stadium-light directions, while buildings and surroundings collapse into featureless black shapes. In `crops/legacy/R04-slot_C.png`, bright body edges and soft ground blobs do little to anchor feet or equipment. `legacy/desktop/R18.png` adds obvious rain streaks, but `crops/legacy/R18-turf_26_67_40.png` still looks dry and matte. `axis-3.jpg` offers stronger grounding and surface definition; `cfb26-3.jpg` demonstrates a much more convincing relationship between illuminated field, crowd, architecture, and night exposure.

3. **Animation & game feel — 3.5/10.** In `strips/legacy/R11.png`, the runner moves laterally and upfield with an upright torso and repeated leg swings, with little visible preparation or weight transfer for the cut. `strips/legacy/R07.png` does not communicate a strong planted throw and follow-through; `crops/legacy/R07-slot_QB.png` also places the ball down beside the QB's feet rather than in a readable throwing grip. `strips/legacy/R10.png` moves from a crowded upright run into a downed player over the later sampled panels without a convincing sustained contact-and-fall beat. `strips/legacy/R13.png` shows a congested upright contact cluster with weak separation between blocking, wrapping, and tackling. `crops/legacy/R12-carrier.png` confirms ground penetration and a ball suspended above the bodies. The camera change for replays deserves credit. The balanced limbs and explicit physical relationships in `cfb26-4.jpg` and `madden26-4.jpg` are useful pose benchmarks; their still images cannot establish animation smoothness. The strips support criticism of staging and contact, but cannot precisely measure frame pacing.

4. **UI / HUD — 6/10.** `legacy/desktop/R04.png` has a coherent team-colored scorebug, differentiated receiver symbols, route art, and an unmistakable Hike action. `legacy/desktop/R21.png` gives the first-down result a clear banner and continuation control. These are above merely functional. However, the persistent sidebar consumes substantial width while leaving large empty areas during live play and results, as in `legacy/desktop/R10.png` and `legacy/desktop/R21.png`. Results are repeated in the field banner, sidebar heading, and log. `legacy/desktop/R05.png` supplies defensive assignments only in the small diagram, leaving the actual formation comparatively uninformative. `legacy/desktop/R22.png` ends with a partially visible play card and cramped descriptions. `axis-1.jpg` integrates assignments and identities more directly into play; `madden26-3.jpg` uses screen space and broadcast hierarchy more deliberately.

5. **Typography — 5.5/10.** Scores and primary actions are easy to identify in `legacy/desktop/R04.png`. The result headline in `legacy/desktop/R21.png` has useful separation from its subtitle. Nevertheless, condensed display lettering, serif descriptions, thin log timestamps, keyboard keycaps, and slab-like result lettering do not form a sufficiently controlled hierarchy. `legacy/desktop/R22.png` squeezes descriptions into small, low-contrast lines beside tiny diagrams, while `legacy/mobile/R21.png` repeats the result at two competing scales. The issue is inconsistent sizing and secondary-text treatment, rather than wholesale illegibility. `madden26-3.jpg` has more disciplined numerical hierarchy; `legendbowl-4.jpg` demonstrates that distinctive typography can remain stylistically unified.

6. **Readability — 5/10.** `legacy/desktop/R10.png` uses a nameplate and white ring effectively to locate the carrier, and the blue and yellow field lines are clear. In `legacy/mobile/R04.png`, however, receiver badges and thick route graphics dominate tiny players; the outer assignments run close to the viewport edges. `legacy/mobile/R08.png` shows a dark airborne ball without a strong contrasting flight cue, surrounded by small players and a large amount of unused distant field. `legacy/desktop/R09.png` does not make the intended catching action immediately apparent. The ball is visible when searched for, but visibility is not the same as instant comprehension. `axis-1.jpg` gives players and assignments more useful scale, while `legendbowl-2.jpg` and `retrobowl-1.jpg` establish more distinct targets and action silhouettes.

7. **Mobile — 5.5/10.** This is a real responsive layout: `legacy/mobile/R22.png` turns play selection into large two-column cards, and `legacy/mobile/R10.png` provides a broad Juke button below the field. `legacy/mobile/R21.png` keeps the score, result, and Continue action legible. The main weakness is the camera: `legacy/mobile/R04.png` spends extensive vertical space on sky, background, and empty turf while shrinking the formation. The run view is somewhat closer but still makes individual contacts difficult to inspect. Large nameplates can obscure nearby bodies. The brief's supplied phone-proxy performance exceeds the 30 fps requirement; that earns performance credit, not visual credit. `retrobowl-1.jpg` and `retrobowl-2.jpg` prioritize action scale more successfully. Landscape mobile framing and actual touch behavior are not demonstrated by these portrait captures.

8. **Authenticity — 5.5/10.** `legacy/desktop/R03.png` supplies Wolves branding, a modest venue building, a huddle, officials, and school-scale staging. `legacy/desktop/R20.png` changes school colors, field identity, and bleacher arrangement for Jessieville. `crops/legacy/R01-board.png` presents a suitably modest physical scoreboard. Those details exceed generic football. However, `legacy/desktop/R15.png` exposes sparse, uniformly arranged sideline figures, crude bleachers, black triangular trees, and little recognizable community activity. No convincing band or differentiated student section is identifiable in the inspected views. The surrounding track-like bands lack convincing lane and venue detail. `cfb26-1.jpg` and `cfb26-2.jpg` are college-scale analogues for organized school identity and event activity, not capacity targets; `legendbowl-4.jpg` also makes its venue feel more inhabited. These captures do not justify certifying exact NFHS compliance.
